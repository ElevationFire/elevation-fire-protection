October in Denver means the first hard freezes are coming. For commercial buildings with fire sprinkler systems, that window between summer and the first sustained cold snap is the most important time of year for one specific category of maintenance. Miss it, and you can end up with burst pipes, water damage, and a system that's offline right when it's needed most.

This isn't a complicated process, but it does require actually doing it before the freeze arrives, not after.

## Why Winter Is Different for Sprinkler Systems

A wet pipe sprinkler system holds pressurized water in the distribution piping at all times. That water freezes at 32 degrees Fahrenheit, and when it does, the pipe fails. A freeze rupture in a wet pipe sprinkler system releases water wherever the break occurs, which is frequently in attic spaces, unheated mechanical rooms, or exterior wall cavities where the damage can spread unseen for hours before anyone notices.

The Denver metro gets its first hard freeze somewhere in October most years, and Front Range weather is fast-moving. A warm week followed by a sudden overnight drop to 20 degrees is a normal fall pattern here. Sprinkler systems that weren't prepped in time are the ones that fail.

## Dry Pipe Low-Point Drains

If your building has a dry pipe zone protecting a parking garage, loading dock, unheated warehouse section, or exterior canopy, the single most important pre-winter task is draining the low points in that piping.

Dry pipe systems hold pressurized air or nitrogen instead of water, but water still enters the system in small amounts over time. It comes from condensation inside the pipes and from minor valve weeping at the dry pipe valve. That water settles at the low points in the piping. When temperatures drop below freezing, it freezes there.

A dry pipe system with a frozen low point is not protecting the space it's designed to cover. Under [NFPA 25](https://www.nfpa.org/codes-and-standards/nfpa-25-standard-for-the-inspection-testing-and-maintenance-of-water-based-fire-protection-systems/about), low-point drains on dry systems should be checked and drained before heating season begins. This is one of the most commonly skipped pre-winter maintenance items on mixed-system buildings.

## Antifreeze Loop Glycol Concentration

If your building has an antifreeze loop protecting a small area where wet pipe would otherwise freeze, such as a loading dock vestibule or an enclosed stairwell on an exterior wall, that loop's glycol concentration needs to be tested before cold weather.

[NFPA 25](https://www.nfpa.org/codes-and-standards/nfpa-25-standard-for-the-inspection-testing-and-maintenance-of-water-based-fire-protection-systems/about) requires the freeze protection level in antifreeze loops to be verified annually. The acceptable freeze protection point varies based on the minimum expected temperature at the specific location the loop protects, with a required safety margin below that temperature. A glycol solution that was correct two years ago may be diluted today from previous work on the system.

Testing is straightforward with a refractometer, but it requires knowing the minimum design temperature for the space and verifying that the current concentration actually provides protection to that level. If the concentration is off, the loop needs to be corrected before temperatures drop.

## Heat Trace and Pipe Insulation

Some Denver commercial buildings protect wet pipe runs in partially conditioned spaces with electric heat trace tape or heat cable rather than converting those zones to dry pipe. Heat trace keeps the pipe above freezing using a low-wattage resistance heating element wrapped around or alongside the pipe.

Before heating season, verify that every section of heat trace in your building is energized and functional. Heat trace that's failed silently over the summer provides no protection when temperatures drop. Walk the accessible sections and check that the thermostat controls are set correctly and that the systems are receiving power. If any sections are not accessible for a visual check, a licensed electrical contractor or fire protection contractor should verify functionality.

Also check insulation on pipes running in unheated areas. Insulation that has been damaged, compressed, or removed during other work reduces the buffer between ambient temperature and the pipe. Gaps in insulation are worth repairing before they become failures.

## Fire Riser Room and Control Room Temperatures

The fire riser room, where your control valves, dry pipe valve, backflow assembly, and alarm check valve are located, needs to stay above 40 degrees Fahrenheit year-round for wet pipe components to remain functional. In older Denver commercial buildings, riser rooms are sometimes in locations where heating is inconsistent: near loading dock doors, in basement areas with exterior walls, or in mechanical rooms that lose heat when adjacent systems are shut down for seasonal maintenance.

Before cold weather arrives, confirm that the riser room has reliable heat and that the heating source isn't dependent on a system that gets turned off for the season. If the riser room temperature has never been specifically verified, a building automation system setpoint or a simple data logger can tell you whether temperatures are staying in the acceptable range on cold nights.

## Building Envelope Checks Near Sprinkler Piping

Renovation work, HVAC changes, roofing work, and exterior repairs can all create new paths for cold air to reach previously protected piping. A gap in an exterior wall, a new penetration through an insulated ceiling, or a modified HVAC damper that now allows cold air to reach a wet pipe run are all things that show up in winter.

If your building had any significant construction or repair work done since last winter, walk the areas where wet pipe runs through or near the building envelope and look for new openings, disturbed insulation, or changed conditions that weren't there before.

## What to Schedule Now

The tasks above are time-sensitive: they need to be done before the first hard freeze, not after the first problem. Scheduling a pre-winter systems check with a licensed fire protection contractor in September or October gives you time to identify and correct issues before temperatures drop.
