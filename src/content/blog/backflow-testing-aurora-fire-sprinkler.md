Backflow prevention is one of the most consistently misunderstood compliance requirements for commercial fire sprinkler systems in Colorado. Property owners know they're supposed to do it annually, but the specifics — which device needs testing, who tests it, where the results go, and what happens if you miss a year — vary by water provider, and Aurora Water operates differently than Denver Water.

If you own or manage a commercial property in Aurora with a fire sprinkler system, here's what you need to know about how backflow testing works in that jurisdiction.

## Why Fire Sprinkler Systems Need Backflow Prevention

A fire sprinkler system connects directly to the domestic water supply. The water in that system sits in the pipes for extended periods, can contain corrosion inhibitors, antifreeze compounds, or biological growth, and operates at different pressures than the municipal supply. Without a backflow prevention device, there's a pathway for that water to flow backwards into the drinking water system under certain pressure conditions.

Backflow prevention assemblies, specifically the type required for fire sprinkler connections in commercial buildings, are designed to block that reverse flow. They are mechanical devices with internal check valves, and like all mechanical devices, they degrade over time. Annual testing verifies that the device is still functioning correctly.

[NFPA 25](https://www.nfpa.org/codes-and-standards/nfpa-25-standard-for-the-inspection-testing-and-maintenance-of-water-based-fire-protection-systems/about) requires backflow prevention assemblies serving fire sprinkler systems to be inspected, tested, and maintained annually. The testing interval isn't negotiable — it's a code requirement.

## How Aurora Water Handles Backflow Compliance

Aurora Water manages its own backflow prevention program independently of Denver Water's program. The two systems have similar requirements in many respects but differ in several practical ways that affect how compliance works for Aurora property owners.

**Approved testers.** Aurora Water maintains its own list of certified backflow assembly testers who are authorized to submit test reports to their system. Not every tester certified to work in Denver is approved by Aurora Water. If your fire protection contractor doesn't appear on Aurora Water's approved tester list, their test results won't be accepted, and you'll be out of compliance even though the physical testing was done correctly.

**Test report submission.** Aurora Water requires test results to be submitted directly to their backflow program, not held by the property owner or submitted on request. The testing contractor submits the report, and Aurora Water tracks compliance through their own system. If a report isn't received by Aurora Water within the required period after the test date, the assembly shows as non-compliant in their records regardless of whether the physical test passed.

**Compliance notifications.** Aurora Water sends compliance notices to property owners when backflow test reports are overdue or when a device has failed and needs repair. These notices come from Aurora Water's water quality program, not from the Aurora Fire Department, which sometimes creates confusion about whether the notice is regulatory or advisory in nature. It's regulatory. A device that fails testing must be repaired and retested before the assembly is considered compliant.

**Device types.** Most commercial fire sprinkler connections in Aurora use a Reduced Pressure Zone (RPZ) assembly or a Double Check Valve Assembly (DCVA). Which type is required depends on the degree of hazard at the connection and Aurora Water's specific requirements for the building's occupancy and use. If you've had work done on your fire line or water service and aren't certain which device type is currently installed, that's worth confirming.

## Common Backflow Compliance Issues in Aurora

A few situations come up repeatedly when working with Aurora commercial properties on backflow compliance.

**Tests submitted to the wrong jurisdiction.** Buildings near the Aurora and Denver border sometimes have testing contractors who submit results to Denver Water instead of Aurora Water, or vice versa. Aurora Water won't have a record of the test, the property shows as non-compliant, and the property owner doesn't find out until they receive a notice. When you schedule your annual test, confirm explicitly that the contractor will submit to Aurora Water.

**Failed devices that weren't repaired.** An RPZ assembly that fails its annual test is not compliant until it's repaired and retested. Some property managers receive a failed test report from their contractor and assume that reporting the failure is sufficient. It isn't. Aurora Water requires a passing test report within a defined period after a failure. The timeline for repair is typically short.

**Missing records from previous owners.** Commercial properties that have changed hands sometimes have no history of backflow testing in the Aurora Water system, either because the previous owner used an unapproved tester, submitted reports to the wrong jurisdiction, or simply never tested. If you acquired an Aurora commercial property recently and can't locate backflow testing records, the safest step is to contact Aurora Water directly and confirm whether your device has an active compliance record.

**New fire service connections.** If your building has had any work done on the fire service line, including underground repairs, meter replacement, or service upgrades, verify with your fire protection contractor that the backflow assembly was properly reinstated and tested after the work was completed. Work on the fire service line can require the assembly to be retested regardless of where you are in the annual testing cycle.

## What the Annual Test Covers

A licensed backflow assembly tester will shut down the fire service, attach test gauges to the assembly's test cocks, and perform a differential pressure test across each check valve and the relief valve on an RPZ assembly. The test verifies that each internal component is holding the required pressure differential and that the relief valve opens and closes correctly.

The test takes roughly 30 to 45 minutes for a standard assembly. It requires the fire service to be temporarily out of service during testing, which counts as an impairment under NFPA 25 and should be coordinated accordingly. For most commercial properties the duration is short enough that formal fire watch isn't triggered, but the impairment should be documented.

## Scheduling Before the Year Closes

If your Aurora commercial property's annual backflow test hasn't been completed yet this year, the fourth quarter is the time to schedule it. Aurora Water's compliance tracking runs on a calendar year for most properties, and tests completed close to the year-end deadline can face reporting delays that push the submission past the compliance window.
